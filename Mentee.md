# MONDAY MEETING — Discussion Checklist

_Things she and I need to talk through and agree together._

**1. Her goals (the engine)**
- [ ] What are her half-year goals, at a high level? (I'm not setting them — just understanding them so I can steer toward them.)
- [ ] Which of them, if any, does she want mentoring help with specifically?
- [ ] Is "Level 1 technical competence" the right destination, or does she see it differently?

**2. What she wants from me**
- [ ] What does she hope to get out of this? (So I'm serving her, not assuming.)
- [ ] How does she learn best — watching, doing, being talked through it, reading?
- [ ] What's worked or not worked with mentors/managers before?

**3. Cadence**
- [ ] Fortnightly scheduled check-in — does that work for her? (Weekly only if she needs it; nothing more.)
- [ ] What day/time suits — and she books it as a recurring calendar slot.
- [ ] Agree the ad-hoc rule: if genuinely stuck, request a slot for _next day_ (not instant), she books it, max 30 mins.

**4. The troubleshooting process ("survive until tomorrow")**
- [ ] Walk her through the draft method (below) — does it make sense to her?
- [ ] Does it feel supportive or restrictive? (Adjust so it's hers, not imposed.)
- [ ] Agree: she comes to check-ins with her _written-down blocker_, not just "I'm stuck."

**5. What each of us brings**
- [ ] Agree her side (her attempt first, a short pre-check-in note) and mine (protected time, honest feedback, signposting).

**6. How we know it's working**
- [ ] Agree a review point (e.g. every 2 months) against her goals.
- [ ] Agree we can both honestly say "this isn't working, let's change it."

**7. Boundaries — framed as help, not constraint**
- [ ] Explain _why_ the structure exists: it's scaffolding to help her focus and build independence, because I'm invested in her doing well — not me being ungenerous.
- [ ] Check she's genuinely comfortable with it, and invite her to push back.

**8. Practical bits**
- [ ] Where do we keep shared notes / track progress?
- [ ] Anything confidential or sensitive to flag (org values, IP handling) as she gets more access?

# DRAFT MENTORING CHARTER

_(To shape together — "here's what I'm thinking, what would you add or change?")_

**Purpose:** To help [mentee] grow into a confident, independent technical practitioner, building both skill and the self-reliance to troubleshoot before escalating. The aim is independence, not dependence. It might feel new and strange but thats where the growth is.

**Her goals drive it:** Her half-year goals are the outcomes we work toward. _(Fill in Monday.)_

**Cadence:**
- Fortnightly scheduled check-in, [day/time], booked by her as a recurring slot.
- Ad-hoc only by exception: if genuinely blocked, request a next-day slot (not instant), book it herself, max 30 mins.
- Next-day-not-instant gives her a chance to work her process first — often she'll have solved it by morning, and that's the win.

**Troubleshooting process (before escalating to me):**
1. Name the type of stuck — knowledge, access, or clarity?
2. Timebox it — a set window of real effort, not endless grinding. 30/60 mins 
3. Can you simplyfy the problem space somehow?
4. Write down 
	1. exactly where I'm stuck — 
	2. what I tried, 
	3. what I saw, 
	4. what I think the cause is. 
	5. What I could try havn't yet _(This is also what I bring to the check-in.)_
5. Check docs / search / a peer/ Teams / Sharepoint / 
6. Still stuck → book the next-day slot, bringing the written blocker.

**What each brings:**
- _She:_ her goals; a short pre-check-in note (worked on / stuck on / want from session); her own first attempt before escalating.
- _Me:_ protected time; honest, constructive feedback; technical guidance and context; signposting within team and org.

**How we know it's working:** Review against her goals every ~2 months. Adjust cadence or scope if needed. Either of us can say "let's change this" without awkwardness.

**Ground rules:**
- Boundaries are for focus, not distance — I'm invested in you doing well.
- Come with your attempt, not just the problem.
- Respect the calendar: book slots, don't ambush.
- Honesty both ways.

---

Two closing steers. First — the checklist is yours to drive the conversation; the charter is the artefact you fill in _during_ it, together. Don't hand her the finished charter and ask her to agree — work through the checklist and let the charter populate as you go. Co-authoring it is what makes her honour it.

Second — the one item most likely to need softening in the room is the ad-hoc boundary. For someone creative and early-career, "next day not instant" can sting if it lands wrong. Lead with the _reason_ (it builds her confidence that she can survive on her own) before the rule, and it'll feel like a gift rather than a wall.
